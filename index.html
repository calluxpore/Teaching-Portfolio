Four convictions

Grounded in constructivist pedagogy, Universal Design for Learning and critical technical inquiry, at the point where craft meets code and human intuition meets machine intelligence.

Learning through making

Design is an action-biased practice, not an abstract theory. In studios such as Interactive Products and Physical Computing, students build working microcontroller circuits, drive physical actuators and test prototypes with external mentors instead of wireframing interactions they never touch. Soldering a sensor and fine-tuning a custom LoRA diffusion model are both forms of thinking. Assessment follows: rubrics weight process documentation, iterative experiments and honest failure at 40 against 60 for the final artefact.

Computation for everyone

Makerspaces and code can intimidate students who do not see themselves as technical. In foundation courses such as Programming 1: Creative Coding and Programming 2: Physical Computation, I code live on the projector, say each decision out loud, make deliberate syntax errors and trace the bugs with the class in real time. Code is treated not as a branch of software engineering but as a material, as plastic as clay or timber.

Questioning as method

I want students to leave with judgement, not ready-made answers. In speculative studios such as Design Fiction and Future Labs, cohorts work with questions that resist quick closure: what does design owe future generations, which communities do current generative models leave out, and how is designing with AI different from using it as a production pipeline? Worldbuilding and diegetic prototyping turn their scepticism into responsible proposals, several of which have gone on to win international awards.

An ethic of care

Teaching is a labour of care. Having taught cohorts in Canada, India, the UK, Germany and Singapore, I plan for different cultural assumptions, languages and ways of learning. High-stakes grading can penalise a student whose thinking is sophisticated but whose English is still developing, so my studios provide modular open-source code scaffolds, bilingual vocabulary aids and steady office hours, in a room built on psychological safety and mutual dignity.
