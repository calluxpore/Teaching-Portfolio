1. Teaching Philosophy (The 4 Pillars)

I teach where design meets computation, craft meets code, and human intuition intersects with machine intelligence. Anchored in constructivist pedagogy, Universal Design for Learning (UDL), and critical technical inquiry, my practice is organized across four foundational commitments:

1. Experiential Learning Through Making

Design is not an abstract theory; it is an action-biased, experiential practice. In studios such as Interactive Products and Physical Computing, students do not simply wireframe conceptual interactions; they build working microcontroller circuits, interface physical actuators, and test prototypes with external mentors. Whether soldering physical sensors or fine-tuning a custom LoRA diffusion model, physical and digital construction is itself a form of rigorous cognitive synthesis. To reflect this, assessment rubrics deliberately weight process documentation, iterative experiments, and unsimulated failure loops in a 40/60 ratio with the final artefact.

2. Radical Accessibility of Computation

Makerspaces, algorithmic scripts, and coding environments can easily intimidate creative students who do not perceive themselves as "technical." Guided by universal design principles, I make computational mediums radically accessible. In foundation coding courses such as Programming 1: Creative Coding and Programming 2: Physical Computation, I use live narrated coding: projecting my editor, vocalizing logic decisions, making deliberate syntax errors, and tracing bugs collaboratively in real time. We frame code not as an austere branch of software engineering, but as an expressive, plastic material like clay or timber.

3. Questioning as Method & Speculative Inquiry

My objective as an educator is not to dispense ready-made solutions, but to cultivate acute analytical discernment. In speculative studios including Design Fiction and Future Labs, I challenge cohorts with provocations that resist rapid closure: What does design owe future generations? Which communities are disenfranchised by current generative models? How does designing with AI differ from merely treating it as a production pipeline? Through structured worldbuilding, dialectical inquiry, and diegetic prototyping, students transform critical skepticism into responsible, award-winning proposals.

4. Global Positionality and the Ethic of Care

Teaching is fundamentally an ongoing, compassionate labour of care. Having taught and mentored diverse cohorts across Canada, India, the UK, Germany, and Singapore, my pedagogy is deeply attuned to varying cultural assumptions, linguistic proficiencies, and learning modalities. Recognizing that rigid, high-stakes grading can penalize students whose thinking is sophisticated but whose English fluency is developing, my studio provides modular open-source code scaffolds, bilingual vocabulary aids, and consistent office-hour mentoring, establishing studio spaces founded on psychological safety, mutual dignity, and lifelong curiosity.
